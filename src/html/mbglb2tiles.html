NAME

mbglb2tiles - make an OGC 3D Tiles tileset of levels of detail from a GLB mesh of mbmesh or mbgrd2gltf

SYNOPSIS

mbglb2tiles -I mesh.glb [-O outputdir] [--tile-size meters] [--weld-tolerance meters] [--threads n] [--no-draco] [--draco-precision meters] [--draco-normal-bits n] [--html [--x3dom-url url] [--sse pixels]] [--cesium-html [--cesium-url url]] [--geoviewpoint lon,lat,elev,clon,clat,celev]... [--lift[=meters]] [--no-colors] [-V] [-h]

DESCRIPTION

mbglb2tiles turns a triangle mesh in a binary glTF (GLB) file written by mbmesh or mbgrd2gltf into an OGC 3D Tiles tileset that a viewer loads a part at a time, at a level of detail that depends on the distance. A mesh of tens of millions of vertices, which a browser cannot decode and hold at once, is shown as the few tiles that are near enough to need their detail and coarser tiles for the rest. Nothing is reconstructed: the mesh is read, coarsened, and cut into tiles.

The GLB must carry the georeference that mbmesh and mbgrd2gltf write in the extras of the asset (georeference, with the vertices either absolute WGS84 ECEF coordinates or relative to a center), because that says where the mesh is on the Earth. Any other GLB is refused. The mesh may have many primitives, as the GLB of mbgrd2gltf and, by default, the mesh.glb of mbmesh have, Draco-compressed (KHR_draco_mesh_compression) or not. A GLB whose vertices are absolute ECEF coordinates in 32-bit floats resolves only about 0.5 m, and the program warns; make the mesh with a GeoOrigin (--geoorigin of mbmesh or mbgrd2gltf).

The program works as follows.

Weld
The primitives of a GLB each have their own copies of the vertices on their edges, each quantized on its own. Vertices closer than the weld tolerance are made one, and the normals are made again from the whole surface, so the shading is smooth across the edges of primitives and tiles.
Frame
The mesh is put in the east-north-up frame at the middle of its bounds. The tiles are in that frame, written Y-up as glTF requires, and the transform of the root tile of tileset.json places it on the Earth.
Levels
The finest level is the mesh. Each level above clusters the vertices of the mesh in cubes whose side is the spacing of the mesh times two to the power of the level, each cluster becoming the mean of its vertices, and drops the triangles that collapse or repeat. The spacing is the square root of the area of the triangles seen from above divided by the number of vertices. The whole mesh is coarsened and then cut, so that the tiles of a level meet exactly along their edges.
Tiles
The tiles of a level are the squares of a lattice, anchored at a corner of the bounds of the mesh, whose side is the tile size times two to the power of the level, and each triangle goes to the tile that has its center. Four tiles of a level are replaced by one of the next level, until one tile holds everything, so every tile has about as many vertices as any other. A tile is a GLB of one primitive, compressed with Draco unless --no-draco is given.
tileset.json
Version 1.1, with the refinement REPLACE, a box bounding volume for each tile that holds its content and its children, and a geometric error that is the cube size of its level (0 for the finest tiles). The tileset has the georeference and provenance of the run in its extras, as the tiles do in the extras of their assets.

OPTIONS

-I mesh.glb, --input mesh.glb
The GLB mesh. Required.
-O outputdir, --output outputdir
Where tileset.json and the tiles go. The directory is created if needed. The tiles are in a directory L0, L1 and so on for each level, L0 being the finest, named tile_I_J.glb after the position of the tile in the lattice of its level. The default is tileset in the directory of the input, which is where mbmesh --tileset writes its tileset, in the same layout. A tileset already in the directory, from either program, is replaced: its tileset.json, Llevel directories and flat tile_I_J.glb files are removed first, and the log says so.
--tile-size meters
The side of the tiles of the finest level. The default is 256 times the spacing of the mesh, to two significant digits, as mbgrd2gltf writes blocks of 256 cells. The side doubles with each level.
--weld-tolerance meters
Vertices of the file this close to each other are one. The default is twice the Draco quantization step that the file records, or 0.001 if that is less.
--threads n
The number of threads that decode the Draco primitives of the input and write the tiles. The default is the number of cores, at most 8.
--no-draco
Write the tiles as arrays, without compression. By default they are compressed with Draco.
--draco-precision meters
With Draco, keep positions to this many meters (default 0.001): each tile gets the bits that make the step of its quantization no more than this over its extent. The same option as mbmesh and mbgrd2gltf take.
--draco-normal-bits n
With Draco, the bits of each component of a normal, 2 to 30 (default 10).
--html
Also write a viewer page, outputdir.html in the directory that holds the output directory, which shows the tileset with an X3DOM GeoTileset node, with a GeoOrigin at the origin of the frame and an overview viewpoint from above. The processing log is in mbglb2tiles.log in the output directory, which the page loads when its log panel is opened. The page must be served over HTTP, as it loads the tiles; the program prints the command that serves it (python3 -m http.server), and does not start it.
--x3dom-url url
The X3DOM script that the --html page loads (default https://www.x3dom.org/x3dom/dist/x3dom-full.js). A GeoTileset needs a build of X3DOM that has it, which that one may not.
--cesium-html
Also write a viewer page for CesiumJS, outputdir_cesium.html in the directory that holds the output directory. The globe of Cesium hides data that are below the ellipsoid, so the page starts with the globe hidden and has a check box that shows it, translucent. The page takes the lift of --lift off again, so that the tileset is at its true height. It must be served over HTTP, like the --html page.
--cesium-url url
The directory of the CesiumJS build that the --cesium-html page loads, with Cesium.js and Widgets/widgets.css in it (default https://cesium.com/downloads/cesiumjs/releases/1.114/Build/Cesium).
--geoviewpoint lon,lat,elev,clon,clat,celev
Add a viewpoint to the Viewpoint menu of the --html and --cesium-html pages; the option can be repeated. The values are geodetic, longitude first, with true heights whatever --lift says: the position of the camera and the point it looks at. Ten numbers, lon,lat,elev,ax,ay,az,angle,clon,clat,celev, give the orientation as well, as an axis and an angle in the local frame (X east, Y up, Z south) as a GeoViewpoint takes it; 1 0 0 -1.5708 looks straight down. The Copy GeoViewpoint button of the X3DOM page and the Copy viewpoint button of the Cesium page give the option for the view on the screen.
--no-colors
Do not color the tiles by height. By default every vertex has a color (COLOR_0) from the Haxby table over the heights of the whole mesh, so that all tiles and levels agree, with a white base color; tileset.json records the bounds as heightColors, and the --html page has a "color by height" checkbox that turns the colors off, which shows the tiles in gray.
--lift[=meters]
Put the tileset above the ellipsoid, along the local vertical, in the transform of tileset.json. As --geoorigin of mbmesh does, without the option there is no lift and the tileset has the true heights (a warning says so if any are below the ellipsoid), the option alone takes the default below, and a value must be attached, as in --lift=900. X3DOM's GeoTileset takes the height of the camera as at least 1 m, so a camera below the ellipsoid, as over the seafloor, never gets close enough to refine a tile and only the root tile loads. The default lift is enough to put the lowest point of the mesh 10 m above the ellipsoid, to a meter, if it is lower. The lift is logged and written as liftMeters in the extras of the tileset, and the viewpoints of the --html page have it added.
--sse pixels
With --html, the maximumScreenSpaceError of the page's GeoTileset: a tile is replaced by its children when its geometric error would show larger than this (default 1; X3DOM's own default of 8 refines tiles of centimeter errors only within a few meters).
-V, --verbose
Log the debug messages as well as the information messages. The log always says what each stage does and which options are in effect, default or not.
-h, --help
Print the usage and exit.

EXAMPLES

Make the tiles of a mesh from mbmesh, and a page to look at them with:
mbmesh -I datalist.mb-1 -L 0.1 --geoorigin
mbglb2tiles -I mbmesh_output/mesh.glb --html
python3 -m http.server 8000 --directory .

Tiles of 50 m at the finest level, not compressed:
mbglb2tiles -I ZTopo.glb --tile-size 50 --no-draco

NOTES

The whole mesh is held in memory, about 100 bytes for each vertex while it is welded and clustered; a mesh of 38 million vertices needed 12 GB. The tiles are in one frame for the whole tileset, which is fine for a survey a few kilometers across. The coarse levels are made by clustering vertices, not by collapsing edges with an error measure, so thin features and sharp edges round off. 3d-tiles-validator reports no errors or warnings for the tilesets, only that it cannot validate the Draco extension.
